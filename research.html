Research

Five interlocking areas, one shared question: how does sound become perception, action, and identity?

Music cognitionAuditory neuroscienceSinging & voice productionVoice & identityComputational modeling

Research area 1

Music Cognition and Perception

How listeners hear pitch, rhythm, harmony, and structure, and how expectations, memory, and experience shape what music means to them.

Guiding questions

  • How do listeners form and update expectations about what comes next in a melody?
  • What distinguishes the perception of music from speech and other sound?
  • How do training and cultural exposure change musical perception?

Methods

  • Behavioral psychophysics
  • Online listening experiments
  • Statistical learning paradigms

Research area 2

Auditory Neuroscience and Neuroimaging

The neural systems that transform acoustic input into perception, studied with electrophysiology and neuroimaging.

Guiding questions

  • How are pitch and timbre represented along the auditory pathway?
  • How are neural prediction signals expressed in event-related potentials?
  • How do auditory and motor systems interact during listening?

Methods

  • EEG / event-related potentials
  • Functional MRI
  • Time-frequency analysis

Research area 3

Singing and Vocal Production

How people plan and control the voice to match a target pitch, and why some find singing accurately harder than others.

Guiding questions

  • How do perception and motor control combine to produce an accurate sung pitch?
  • What does auditory feedback contribute to vocal control?
  • How can singing accuracy be measured robustly outside the laboratory?

Methods

  • Singing accuracy protocols
  • Pitch tracking (YIN)
  • Auditory feedback manipulation

Research area 4

Voice, Identity, and Self-Perception

How people perceive their own voices and others', and how voice relates to identity, gender, and social perception.

Guiding questions

  • How do listeners infer identity and social attributes from voice?
  • How do people perceive and evaluate their own voices?
  • How can voice measures be developed that are valid across diverse groups?

Methods

  • Perceptual rating studies
  • Psychometric scale development
  • Acoustic voice analysis

Research area 5

Computational Modeling and Machine Learning

Formal models and machine learning that make hypotheses about perception and production testable and scalable.

Guiding questions

  • Which statistical models best capture musical expectation?
  • What do learned audio representations capture about voice and music?
  • How can models of individual taste or ability be fit from large online samples?

Methods

  • Probabilistic and information-theoretic models
  • Learned audio embeddings
  • Mixed-effects and Bayesian modeling
