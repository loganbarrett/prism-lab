Explore the Science

Six interactive demonstrations of how we think about sound, brain, voice, and models.

Read this first

Everything below is a demonstration. Waveforms, embeddings, and audio are synthetic and generated in your browser; the brain surface is a standard template, not any participant’s brain. Where a demonstration is parameterized by values from the lab’s own manuscripts, it says so. None of it is a new result, and the models are deliberately simplified.

1. Cortical model2. EEG / ERP explorer3. Voice cues4. Latent space5. Melodic expectancy6. Network

Cortical model

A real cortical surface from the ICBM152 template, parcellated with the AAL atlas. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, click a region, isolate a region to ghost the rest of the cortex, or lower the opacity and cut sagittally to see the thalamus, basal ganglia, insula, and Heschl’s gyrus.

EEG and ERP explorer

Modeled on the lab’s melodic-violation study: the same 40 melodies heard while rating them, watching a silent film, or playing a VR game, with expected (Good) or pitch-violated (Bad) final notes. Waveform shapes are synthetic; each measurement-window mean is set to the value reported in the manuscript (in preparation).

Voice cues and altered feedback

Two cues carry most of the acoustic information listeners use to judge a voice: the fundamental frequency (pitch) and the formants (vocal-tract resonance). Shift each independently, then play and compare. Real-time altered auditory feedback manipulates exactly these cues.

Latent-space explorer

Two illustrative embedding spaces, 8-dimensional synthetic points projected to 3D by PCA. One mirrors the idea that a feminine–masculine direction is shared across speaking and singing descriptions; the other mirrors the finding that coarse voice class, but not fine-grained Fach, is acoustically recoverable. Drag to rotate; arrow keys also work.

Melodic expectancy: wrong note, wrong time

Choose how the final note of a short C-major melody deviates in pitch and in timing, then listen. Endings follow the lab’s expectancy design (five pitch levels crossed with timing shifts at 120 bpm). A toy model adds a pitch surprisal and a timing surprisal; neither is fit to data.

Sources and attributions

  • Cortical surface: ICBM 152 Nonlinear 2009 template (Fonov et al., 2011; Fonov et al., 2009), McGill/MNI. Parcellation: Automated Anatomical Labeling atlas (Tzourio-Mazoyer et al., 2002). Surface extracted and smoothed for display; boundaries are approximate.
  • ERP explorer: paradigm and window means from Barrett, Satkunarajah, Cooke & Zendel, manuscript in preparation; stimuli from Halpern et al. (2017); ERAN source modeling from Maess et al. (2001). Waveform shapes are synthetic.
  • Melodic expectancy: design after Barrett, Sauvé, Halpern & Zendel, manuscript in preparation; tonal hierarchy after Krumhansl & Shepard (1979).
  • Latent space: illustrative synthetic data motivated by the lab’s preprint on speaking and singing voice descriptions (PsyArXiv 4g2xc) and by the Fach classification study of historical recordings.
